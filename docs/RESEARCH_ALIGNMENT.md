# Doctoral Research Alignment

## Research frame

**Working title:** *Organizational Leadership and Narrative World-Building in Independent Game Studio Production*

**Primary research question:** How do leadership and production structures in small, founder-led independent game studios shape—and get shaped by—the narrative worlds they build?

The doctoral project is a longitudinal practice-research study at Loptr Lab. Veiled Dominion is the primary creative case. Duet: Solo is the accessibility artifact: a separate, screen-reader-first prototype through which accessibility practice, experimental mechanics, and founder-led production decisions can be documented and analyzed.

## Repository role

This repository is:

- a two-player, 8×8 accessibility artifact;
- an experimental mechanics lab;
- a production and version-history record;
- distinct from the canonical four-player rules authority in Loptr-Lab/veiled-dominion-engine.

It is not:

- the canonical four-player engine;
- authority for the 14×14 rules;
- a source from which experimental behavior becomes canon automatically.

See docs/CANONICAL_DIVERGENCES.md for the current mechanics boundary.

## Research questions supported here

Duet contributes evidence to the primary question and especially to:

- **RQ2:** How internal narrative frameworks—including lore, factions, mission language, and shared vocabulary—operate as organizational tools under changing teams, resource constraints, and uncertainty.
- **RQ3:** How founder-led creative practice evolves over multiple years and what that evolution reveals about sustainable leadership for solo and founder-driven studios.

As the accessibility artifact, Duet also supports analysis alongside accessibility, creative-industries, independent-production, narrative/world-building, leadership, and practice-research literature.

## Practice-research loop

Repository work should preserve the study's analytic loop:

> Practice / Artifact → Question → Method → Evidence → Analysis → Finding → Contribution

The prototype, commits, design documents, architecture decisions, and changing priorities are evidence. They do not become research findings without subsequent analysis and contextualization.

## Evidence practices

Relevant evidence may include:

- accessible interaction and screen-reader design decisions;
- game and systems prototypes;
- experimental rule changes and their rationale;
- design/world-building and production documents;
- repository history and pull-request discussion;
- pitch and public-facing materials;
- reflexive accounts of founder-led decisions;
- changes caused by time, resource, team, or technical constraints.

Where practical:

1. Use focused branches and descriptive commits.
2. State whether a change is Duet-only, presentation-only, documentation-only, or proposed for canonical review.
3. Record rationale, constraints, alternatives, and unresolved questions in PRs or ADRs.
4. Preserve superseded evidence explicitly rather than silently rewriting its history.
5. Separate observed production evidence from later interpretation and research findings.

## Canon and continuity constraints

- The canonical four-player repository remains the rules authority.
- Duet's fixed DURATION_TURNS: 2 Veil lifecycle is a documented divergence from the canonical owner-turn lifecycle.
- Rebirth becoming Veiled and immediate rebirth_lost_control are experimental.
- Fog Mode is optional and noncanonical.
- Resonance remains presentation language, not a stat, HP system, damage type, or resource.
- Duet code must not be merged into the canonical codebase merely to create apparent consistency.

## Comparative context

The wider doctoral project compares Loptr Lab with approximately two or three independent or founder-led studios. This repository should preserve accurate evidence of Duet's production and accessibility practice; it should not manufacture comparative findings or scholarly conclusions before the planned analysis.
