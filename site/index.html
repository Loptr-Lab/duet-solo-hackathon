Veiled Dominion: Duet

We are analyzing the results of this experiment: what players were able to do, where the implementation fell short, and whether the evidence supports another development phase.

THE GAME SERVER IS SHUT DOWN · THIS PAGE IS THE PROJECT HOME

The Google Cloud trial ended, and we could not responsibly move the game to Azure as a playable rebuild without AI-assisted engineering and a more complete redesign. Rather than present an unverified rebuild as ready, we have kept the game offline while reviewing the evidence and defining what a reliable, accessible version would require.

01 · Why the server is offline

The experiment needs an honest evaluation before another deployment.

The shutdown is a pause for evidence and engineering—not a claim that the idea failed or that nobody played. Cloud Run request activity alone cannot tell us how many people played, finished matches, returned, or encountered broken rules.

Rules and game behavior

Duet is a chess-derived variant, not standard chess. Its custom rules must be tested against the intended design, including piece movement, Veil and Sanctuary, Rebirth/Death interactions, promotion, and consistent behavior between local, remote, and AI play.

Reliable, accessible play

Remote matches, reconnection, persistence, keyboard operation, and screen-reader feedback require real-client acceptance tests. Source code or a passing unit test alone is not proof that the full experience works.

Infrastructure and rebuild

The Google Cloud trial ended. An Azure scaffold was not a complete, verified replacement for the playable game. We will not imply that a migration or redeployment succeeded until it has been tested and explicitly approved.

Evidence before investment

We need to recover available usage records and distinguish genuine play from automated traffic, then decide whether the next step should be focused fixes, AI-assisted engineering, a human development team, or no further rebuild.

02 · What we are evaluating

What did the experiment actually demonstrate?

  • Reach: how many genuine visitors or players can be established from available evidence.
  • Activation: whether a visitor started a game or made meaningful moves.
  • Completion: whether matches reached a recorded outcome.
  • Return interest: evidence of repeat sessions, returning players, or unsolicited feedback.
  • Playability: rule correctness, remote reliability, and keyboard/screen-reader usability.
  • Feasibility: what can be repaired in the existing design versus what needs redesign.

We will report only what the surviving logs, match records, tests, and reviews support. Missing telemetry will be described as unknown—not interpreted as proof of no interest.

03 · Open review

Please review and comment on the rebuild.

We invite players, accessibility testers, chess-variant designers, engineers, and researchers to review the evidence and proposed rebuild. Focused, reproducible observations are especially useful: the rule or interaction, what you expected, what happened, and the steps needed to reproduce it.

Useful review areas: intended variant rules versus implemented rules; accessibility and recovery; evidence quality; and the smallest credible next experiment. Please identify assumptions as assumptions and distinguish confirmed findings from hypotheses.

Project boundaries

A research artifact, with human-led decisions.

Duet is an experimental game and does not automatically establish canon for the wider Veiled Dominion setting. PIXIE is bounded accessibility support for orientation, continuity, and executive-function assistance—not an autonomous player or decision-maker. Humans retain authority over rules, canon, releases, and whether this experiment proceeds.
