What this is

A documented experiment

The Weaver began with a proposed collaboration. It asks what happens when two independent creative systems try to work together without either person disappearing into the other's ownership, identity, jurisdiction, or legacy.

What this is not

A finished agreement

This documentation records a creative and research process. It is not a contract, license, assignment, partnership agreement, legal opinion, or determination of ownership.

The provenance

How the door got here

The project preserves its history rather than rewriting the beginning as inevitable. A proposed collaboration came first; the creative systems and game experiments developed through subsequent work.

The questions

Questions for the collaborator

These questions were developed in connection with the proposed collaboration. They are also available as an open framework for people who want to explore the underlying questions from their own perspective.

  1. What does a fair collaboration actually require?
  2. What happens when two people bring unequal things to the table?
  3. How should ownership and authorship be separated?
  4. What happens when the collaboration crosses jurisdictions?
  5. What happens if the relationship changes?
  6. What happens to the work after one of us dies?
  7. What does independence look like inside collaboration?
  8. What am I failing to see?
  9. What would you build?

An independent response is still independent.

Other people can answer these questions. Their answers do not become a response on behalf of the original collaborator, and answering does not establish agreement with the premises.

Where the door leads

The Weaver is connected to the game.

Duet is the accessibility-built experimental artifact. In the larger system, two decks can become one kingdom — 52 Cards of War — while conflicts between systems can be settled through play. The Weaver is the human and creative layer that asks what it means for those systems to coexist.
